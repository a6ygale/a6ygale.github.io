RESEARCH PORTFOLIO / RESEARCH

Research directions

Research interests at the intersection of applied electromagnetics, computation, and engineered wavefronts.

R / 01

MODEL · ANALYZE · OPTIMIZE

Computational electromagnetics

Computational models provide a way to explore how geometry, material properties, excitation, and boundary conditions influence electromagnetic fields. I am interested in using numerical and analytical tools to make those relationships more understandable and useful in sensor and communication-system design.

Numerical modelingWave propagationOptimization
R / 02

PHASE · GEOMETRY · DIRECTIVITY

Phased arrays & beamforming

Array geometry and element excitation determine how fields combine in the far field. I am interested in beam steering, sidelobe control, null placement, sparse geometries, and optimization methods that balance directivity with robustness.

Array factorSparse arraysAdaptive beamforming

R / 03

SHAPE THE FIELD

Engineered wavefronts

I want to investigate how wavefront structure and unconventional electromagnetic degrees of freedom can be used in communication, wireless security, and high-resolution sensing. Areas of interest include phase-profile design, polarization, orbital angular momentum, and propagation-aware beam shaping.

Phase profilesTHz / sub-THzWavefront shaping
R / 04

FROM RF TO OPTICAL SYSTEMS

Cross-domain sensing

My background spans physics, microscopy, and RF engineering. That combination motivates questions about how field control, signal processing, and computational imaging can improve the information extracted from physical measurements.

Sensor systemsSignal processingComputational imaging
